# Assignment 02 — A composition report someone can audit

**Level:** L3 (independent) · **Weight:** 20% · **Time:** 5–6 hours

## Scenario / Problem statement

A colleague has 60 human protein sequences in a FASTA file and has been asked, in a
grant application, to "describe the composition and length distribution of the
panel". They want three things: numbers they can defend, a figure they can put in
the application, and enough documentation that a reviewer could check it.

You are producing that package. The temptation is to run one command and paste the
output. The task is to produce a report where every number can be traced to code and
input.

## Requirements / Task

1. **Analysis.** For `datasets/sample/uniprot_human_subset.fasta`, report:
   - number of records, total residues, mean and median length, with units;
   - the length distribution, and an explicit statement that the sample is
     *stratified by design* (10 proteins per 100-aa band) so the flat shape is not a
     biological finding;
   - the mean molecular mass in kilodaltons (kDa) with the conversion shown;
   - the five most frequent residues overall, and the two proteins with the highest
     and lowest lysine fraction;
   - one measure of composition bias, correctly defined, with its denominator
     stated.
2. **Independence check.** Recompute three of the numbers a second way (plain
   Python, no `seqreport` statistics functions) and include a table of
   `statistic | method A | method B | difference`.
3. **Figure.** One figure, publication-usable at 300 dpi: labelled axes with units, a
   caption in the figure file's companion Markdown that states what is and is not
   shown, colour-blind-safe palette, no chartjunk. It must be regenerated by a
   script, not drawn by hand.
4. **Reproducible pipeline.** `make_report.py` writes `report.md`, `report.json` and
   `figure.png` from the input path. Running it twice must produce byte-identical
   outputs; demonstrate this.
5. **Caveat section.** In `report.md`, one section titled "What this analysis cannot
   tell you". At least three specific limitations (hints: the panel is not a random
   sample of the human proteome; average mass ignores post-translational
   modifications; composition says nothing about function, localisation or disorder).

## Deliverables

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Points | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| All required statistics, correct, with units | 4 | Matches an independent recomputation; kDa conversion visible |
| Sampling caveat stated | 3 | Says the distribution is stratified by design, not evidence about proteomes |
| Independent recomputation | 4 | Three statistics, genuinely independent method, differences measured |
| Figure quality | 3 | 300 dpi, both axes labelled with units, caption states what is not shown |
| Determinism demonstrated | 3 | Hashes identical across runs; command shown |
| Limitations section | 3 | Three specific, non-generic limitations, each tied to a claim in the report |

## Reproducibility requirement

From a fresh clone, `python make_report.py datasets/sample/uniprot_human_subset.fasta`
must regenerate every artefact, and `git diff --exit-code` on the outputs must pass.

## Integrity and attribution

- AI assistance is permitted with one line of disclosure in `report.md` saying which
  parts were assisted.
- Cite data provenance (UniProt, accessed 2026-11-06, CC BY 4.0) — this is a
  **licensing** obligation, and is required here because the data are third-party.
  Nothing else needs citing, and no marks are awarded for citations.
- Fabricated numbers are an automatic zero, and fabricated numbers *with* a
  reproducibility transcript attached are treated more seriously than a plain zero.

## Extension (bonus)

Add a second panel to the figure: mass versus length, with a note explaining why the
relationship is almost linear and where it is not.
